\begin{abstract}
Sparse attention methods such as Loki cut the cost of decoding by ranking the
tokens in the KV-cache with attention scores computed in a low-dimensional
space, and then computing exact attention only for the top-$k$ tokens.
The quality of such a method rests entirely on one offline choice: the basis
used to shrink queries and keys before scoring.
Prior methods pick this basis by principal component analysis (PCA) of the keys,
which minimizes key \emph{reconstruction} error.
We show that this is the wrong objective.
The quantity that matters for top-$k$ selection is the error in the
\emph{score} $\bq^\top\bk$, and we prove that, under a Gaussian surrogate, the
expected squared score error decomposes into a cross-covariance
\emph{alignment} term plus a coupled \emph{fluctuation} term.
Key-PCA minimizes a degenerate case of the second term and ignores the first.
We propose \method{}, which builds a single shared orthonormal basis from the
left singular vectors of the query--key cross-covariance $\E[\bk\bq^\top]$
and is the exact minimizer of the natural surrogate of the alignment term.
For grouped-query attention we show that pooling the cross-covariance across
the query heads of a group is the exact minimizer of the group-summed objective,
so \method{} needs no more bases than Loki and has identical runtime cost.

the perplexity gap to full attention from \TODO{$x$} to \TODO{$y$} at the same
compression ratio, with \TODO{downstream accuracy summary}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autoregressive decoding with a large language model (LLM) must read the entire
key--value (KV) cache at every generation step. At long context this cache can
be larger than the model weights, and the cost of moving it from DRAM into
registers dominates the attention step \citep{loki,sparq}. One popular way to
reduce this cost without retraining is \emph{sparse attention by selection}:
compute cheap approximate scores for every cached token, pick the top-$k$
tokens, and run exact attention only on those. Because no token is ever
deleted, selection avoids the permanent information loss of eviction methods
\citep{h2o,streamingllm}.

Loki \citep{loki} made selection practical by observing that attention keys
have low effective rank. It fits PCA to keys from a calibration set, rotates
queries and keys into that basis, and scores tokens using only the first $r$
of $d$ coordinates. This works because PCA keeps the directions in which keys
have the most energy, so the truncated keys stay close to the original keys.

This paper starts from a simple point that is easy to miss:
\emph{the selector never needs the keys to be close; it needs the scores to be
close.} The score is a bilinear form $\bq^\top\bk$. A direction in key space
that holds a lot of key energy contributes nothing to the score if queries
never point along it. Conversely, a direction with little key energy can
decide the ranking if queries consistently probe it. Key-PCA cannot see this
distinction because it never looks at the queries. Figure~\ref{fig:toy} gives
a two-dimensional example in which key-PCA collapses two keys that every
query separates cleanly.

We make this intuition precise. We write down the expected squared score error
of a rank-$r$ shared-basis approximation and show that, under a joint-Gaussian
surrogate, it splits into two parts (Theorem~\ref{thm:wick}): a
\emph{residual alignment} term that depends only on the query--key
cross-covariance $\bC=\E[\bk\bq^\top]$, and a \emph{coupled fluctuation} term
that depends on the two auto-covariances through their product. Key-PCA
minimizes the fluctuation term with the queries assumed isotropic and the
alignment term dropped entirely. We then propose \method{}, whose basis is the
top-$r$ left singular subspace of $\bC$. It is the exact minimizer of the
natural Frobenius surrogate of the alignment term (Theorem~\ref{thm:ey}), it
recovers Loki's basis exactly when queries are replaced by keys
(Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}), and it preserves inner products exactly at full
rank (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact}). For grouped-query attention (GQA) we show that
pooling $\bC$ over the query heads in a group is the exact minimizer of the
group-summed surrogate (Theorem~\ref{thm:gqa}), so \method{} stores exactly one
basis per KV head, the same as Loki.

\method{} changes only the offline calibration statistic. The online algorithm,
the memory layout, the kernels, and therefore the runtime cost are identical
to Loki. Every accuracy gain we report is attributable to a single design
choice.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item A decomposition of the expected squared score error of shared-basis
        sparse attention into an alignment term and a fluctuation term, with
        a mapping of Loki, SALS, SWAN and SVDq onto degenerate cases
        (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).
  \item \method{}: a closed-form basis from the cross-covariance
        $\E[\bk\bq^\top]$ that is surrogate-optimal for the alignment term,
        with a GQA-pooled construction that is exactly optimal for the
        group-summed surrogate (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item A per-head \emph{alignment ratio} $\rho_h$ that predicts where
        \method{} should beat key-PCA, and an offline oracle basis that
        minimizes the full objective, used to measure how much of the
        available gain \method{} captures (Sections~\ref{sec:rho},~\ref{sec:oracle}).
  \item An evaluation on \TODO{models} showing higher attention-mass recall,
        lower attention-output error, and a smaller perplexity gap than Loki
        at matched compression, with \TODO{downstream tasks}
        (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Attention.}
Consider one attention head with head dimension $d$. When generating token
$S$, the head computes
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:attn}
  \mathbf{y} \;=\; \mathrm{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{\bq^\top \bK^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\Big)\bV,
\end{equation}
where $\bq\in\R^{d}$ is the current query and $\bK,\bV\in\R^{S\times d}$ hold
the keys and values of all $S$ tokens seen so far. Modern models apply rotary
position embeddings (RoPE) \citep{rope} to $\bq$ and $\bk$ before the dot
product. We write $\bq,\bk$ for the vectors that actually enter the dot
product and say explicitly when we mean pre-RoPE states.

\paragraph{Grouped-query attention.}
In GQA \citep{gqa}, a group of $G$ query heads shares one key head and one
value head. We index query heads in a group by $g\in\{1,\dots,G\}$ and write
$\bq^{(g)}$ for their queries; all of them score against the same $\bk$.

\paragraph{Top-$k$ selection.}
Selection methods compute an approximate score $\tilde s_j$ for every cached
token $j$, keep the set $\mathcal{T}$ of the $k$ largest, and compute
\eqref{eq:attn} restricted to $\mathcal{T}$. Loki obtains $\tilde s_j$ by
rotating $\bq$ and $\bk_j$ into a PCA basis of the keys and using only the
first $r$ coordinates, so the approximate scoring costs $O(Sr)$ instead of
$O(Sd)$, and the exact part costs $O(kd)$.

\subsection{Related Work}

\paragraph{Low-rank keys and latent scoring.}
Loki \citep{loki} showed that attention keys have low effective rank across
many models and datasets, and used a per-head key-PCA basis for latent
scoring. SALS \citep{sals} stores keys in a joint-head key-PCA basis and
reconstructs only the selected keys, adding memory compression. SWAN
\citep{swan} builds the basis from $\E[\bq\bq^\top]+\E[\bk\bk^\top]$. SVDq
\citep{svdq} uses the key-PCA spectrum to allocate mixed precision across
latent coordinates. All of these derive the basis from second moments of
queries or keys separately; none uses the joint statistic $\E[\bk\bq^\top]$.
Section~\ref{sec:theory} shows that each of them minimizes a degenerate case
of the score-error objective.

\paragraph{Query-aware dimension selection.}
SparQ \citep{sparq} picks, per query, the coordinates where $|\bq|$ is
largest and scores keys on those coordinates. It is query-aware but works in
the standard coordinate system and needs non-contiguous column gathers; it
also keeps two copies of the keys. \method{} is query-aware through
calibration statistics, uses a fixed rotated basis, and keeps Loki's
contiguous slicing.

\paragraph{Cross-covariance in attention.}
Lambda Networks \citep{lambda} contract positions first and express attention
as a $d\times d$ matrix built from keys and values; the object
$\bK^\top\bQ$ appears there as a \emph{trained replacement} for attention.
\method{} uses $\bK^\top\bQ$ only as an offline calibration statistic for a
post-hoc selector; the model is unchanged.

\paragraph{Eviction and compression.}
StreamingLLM \citep{streamingllm}, H2O \citep{h2o}, Scissorhands
\citep{scissorhands}, KeyDiff \citep{keydiff} and Ada-KV \citep{adakv}
permanently drop tokens. ShadowKV \citep{shadowkv} keeps a low-rank pre-RoPE
key store on GPU and exact values on CPU. Quantization of the KV cache
\citep{kivi} is complementary. \method{} is a pure selection method: the full
cache is retained and one dimension reduction is used for scoring only.

\section{Why Key Energy Is the Wrong Target}
\label{sec:analysis}

Loki's motivating analysis measures how much key variance the top-$r$
principal components capture. We repeat that analysis and add the quantity
that actually controls selection: how much \emph{score} the same subspace
captures. This section is empirical; Section~\ref{sec:theory} gives the
theory.

\subsection{Setup}

Fix a layer and head. From a calibration set of $N$ tokens we collect
queries $\bQ\in\R^{N\times d}$ and keys $\bK\in\R^{N\times d}$ (both taken at
the point where they enter the dot product; see
Section~\ref{sec:rope} for pre- vs post-RoPE). We form three second-moment
matrices:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:moments}
  \Sk = \tfrac1N \bK^\top\bK,\qquad
  \Sq = \tfrac1N \bQ^\top\bQ,\qquad
  \bC = \tfrac1N \bK^\top\bQ \;\approx\; \E[\bk\bq^\top].
\end{equation}
$\Sk$ and $\Sq$ are symmetric positive semidefinite; $\bC$ is square but
generally not symmetric. Loki's basis is the eigenbasis of $\Sk$.

For any rank-$r$ orthonormal $\bU\in\R^{d\times r}$ we define two capture
ratios:
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:capture}
  \mathrm{KeyEnergy}_r(\bU) &= \frac{\tr(\bU^\top\Sk\bU)}{\tr(\Sk)},&
  \mathrm{ScoreEnergy}_r(\bU) &= \frac{\fro{\bU^\top\bC}^2}{\fro{\bC}^2}.
\end{align}
The first is Loki's ``explained variance''. The second is the fraction of
squared cross-covariance retained by $\bU$; Theorem~\ref{thm:ey} shows it is
the quantity a score-faithful basis should maximize.

\begin{definition}[Alignment ratio]
\label{def:rho}
For a head $h$ with moments $\bC_h,\Sq[,h],\Sk[,h]$, define
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:rho}
  \rho_h \;=\; \frac{\fro{\bC_h}^2}{\tr(\Sq[,h])\,\tr(\Sk[,h])/d}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
$\rho_h$ compares the systematic query--key alignment with the product of
the two total energies, normalized so that independent isotropic $\bq,\bk$
give $\rho_h\approx 1/d$ up to sampling noise. Theorem~\ref{thm:wick}
explains why $\rho_h$ predicts the advantage of \method{} over key-PCA.

\section{Theory: Score-Faithful Bases}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Setup and the exact objective}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $\bU\in\R^{d\times r}$ have orthonormal columns, $\bU^\top\bU=\bI_r$.
Define the projector onto its span and its complement,
\begin{equation}
  \bP=\bU\bU^\top,\qquad \bM=\bI-\bP,\qquad \bP^2=\bP,\;\bM^2=\bM,\;\tr\bM=d-r.
\end{equation}
A \emph{shared-basis} approximation scores a token by
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:latent}
  \tilde s(\bq,\bk) = (\bU^\top\bq)^\top(\bU^\top\bk) = \bq^\top\bP\bk,
\end{equation}
so the score error is $s-\tilde s = \bq^\top\bk-\bq^\top\bP\bk = \bq^\top\bM\bk$.
Loki, SALS, SWAN and \method{} all have this form at inference time; they
differ only in how $\bU$ is chosen offline. The natural target is the
expected squared score error over the joint distribution of $(\bq,\bk)$
induced by the calibration corpus:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:J}
  J(\bU) \;=\; \E\big[(\bq^\top\bM\bk)^2\big].
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Why $J$ and not ranking loss]
Top-$k$ selection depends on the order of scores, not their values. But the
ordering is a discontinuous function of the scores, and any bound on ranking
error must go through the score error. $J$ is the smallest smooth objective
that controls it: a token with true score $s_j$ and approximate score
$\tilde s_j$ leaves the top-$k$ set only if $|s_j-\tilde s_j|$ exceeds its
margin to the $k$-th score. Section~\ref{sec:results} shows empirically that
lower $J$ tracks higher attention-mass recall, lower attention-output error
and lower perplexity.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Two elementary properties}

\begin{lemma}[Exactness at full rank; failure of the asymmetric variant]
\label{lem:exact}
Let $\bU\in\R^{d\times d}$ be orthogonal. Then $\bq^\top\bP\bk=\bq^\top\bk$
for all $\bq,\bk$. In contrast, for any two orthogonal
$\bU,\bV\in\R^{d\times d}$, the asymmetric score
$(\bV^\top\bq)^\top(\bU^\top\bk)=\bq^\top\bV\bU^\top\bk$ equals
$\bq^\top\bk$ for all $\bq,\bk$ if and only if $\bV=\bU$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $\bU$ is square and orthogonal then $\bP=\bU\bU^\top=\bI$ and the first
claim is immediate. For the second, $\bq^\top\bV\bU^\top\bk=\bq^\top\bk$ for
all $\bq,\bk$ means $\bV\bU^\top=\bI$, i.e.\ $\bV=\bU$ since $\bU$ is
orthogonal. Otherwise $\bV\bU^\top$ is a non-identity rotation and there
exist $\bq,\bk$ with $\bq^\top\bV\bU^\top\bk\neq\bq^\top\bk$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:exact} explains a negative result we obtained early on. The
singular value decomposition $\bC=\bU\bm{\Sigma}\bV^\top$ offers two natural
factors, and the tempting design is to project keys with $\bU$ and queries
with $\bV$. This does not converge to exact attention as $r\to d$, because the
full-rank map $\bV\bU^\top$ is a rotation, not the identity. Experimentally
its recall sat at the random-guessing floor. The deployable object is the
\emph{shared} left singular subspace, for which $\bP\to\bI$ as $r\to d$.

\begin{lemma}[Pointwise score-error bound]
\label{lem:pointwise}
For any $\bq,\bk$ and projector $\bM$,
$|\bq^\top\bM\bk| \le \norm{\bM\bq}\,\norm{\bM\bk}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\bq^\top\bM\bk=(\bM\bq)^\top(\bM\bk)$ since $\bM=\bM^\top=\bM^2$; apply
Cauchy--Schwarz.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:pointwise} is the analogue of Loki's Lemma~4.2. Key-PCA
minimizes $\E\norm{\bM\bk}^2=\tr(\bM\Sk)$, i.e.\ the second factor only. The
bound is loose in exactly the situation of Figure~\ref{fig:toy}: the residual
key is large but orthogonal to every residual query.

\subsection{Decomposition of the objective}
\label{sec:wick}

\begin{assumption}[Gaussian surrogate]
\label{ass:gauss}
$(\bq,\bk)$ are jointly Gaussian with zero mean, cross-covariance
$\bC=\E[\bk\bq^\top]$ and auto-covariances $\Sq=\E[\bq\bq^\top]$,
$\Sk=\E[\bk\bk^\top]$.
\end{assumption}

The zero-mean part is a convenience; Remark~\ref{rem:mean} handles means.
The Gaussian part is used only to close the fourth moment; the first term of
the decomposition below holds without it.

\begin{theorem}[Alignment--fluctuation decomposition]
\label{thm:wick}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:gauss},
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:wick}
  J(\bU)
  = \underbrace{(\tr\bM\bC)^2 + \tr(\bM\bC\bM\bC)}_{\Jal(\bU)}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\tr(\bM\Sq\bM\Sk)}_{\Jfl(\bU)} .
\end{equation}
Without the Gaussian assumption, $J(\bU)=(\tr\bM\bC)^2+\mathrm{Var}(\bq^\top\bM\bk)$
still holds; only the expression for the variance uses Gaussianity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $\bq^\top\bM\bk=\sum_{i,j}M_{ij}q_ik_j$. Then
\[
J=\sum_{i,j,a,b}M_{ij}M_{ab}\,\E[q_ik_jq_ak_b].
\]
By Isserlis' theorem for zero-mean Gaussians,
$\E[q_ik_jq_ak_b]=\E[q_ik_j]\E[q_ak_b]+\E[q_iq_a]\E[k_jk_b]+\E[q_ik_b]\E[q_ak_j]$.
Using $C_{ji}=\E[k_jq_i]$, $(\Sq)_{ia}=\E[q_iq_a]$, $(\Sk)_{jb}=\E[k_jk_b]$,
the three pairings give:
\begin{align*}
  \sum M_{ij}C_{ji}\sum M_{ab}C_{ba} &= (\tr\bM\bC)^2,\\
  \sum_{i,j,a,b} M_{ij}(\Sq)_{ia}M_{ab}(\Sk)_{jb}
   &= \sum_{j,b}\big(\bM^\top\Sq\bM\big)_{jb}(\Sk)_{jb}
    = \tr(\bM\Sq\bM\Sk),\\
  \sum_{i,j,a,b} M_{ij}C_{bi}M_{ab}C_{ja}
   &= \sum_{i,a}\Big(\sum_j M_{ij}C_{ja}\Big)\Big(\sum_b M_{ab}C_{bi}\Big)
    = \sum_{i,a}(\bM\bC)_{ia}(\bM\bC)_{ai}
    = \tr(\bM\bC\bM\bC),
\end{align*}
where we used $\bM=\bM^\top$ and $\Sk=\Sk^\top$ in the second line.
For the non-Gaussian statement, $\E[\bq^\top\bM\bk]=\tr(\bM\bC)$ and
$\E[X^2]=(\E X)^2+\mathrm{Var}X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Non-zero means]
\label{rem:mean}
If $\bq=\bar\bq+\bq'$, $\bk=\bar\bk+\bk'$ with centered $\bq',\bk'$, then
$\bq^\top\bM\bk=\bar\bq^\top\bM\bar\bk+\bar\bq^\top\bM\bk'+\bq'^\top\bM\bar\bk+\bq'^\top\bM\bk'$.
The uncentered second moment $\E[\bk\bq^\top]=\bar\bk\bar\bq^\top+\bC'$ that
we compute in practice therefore already includes the mean product, and the
extra cross terms are of the form $\bar\bq^\top\bM\,\bk'$, which the Frobenius
surrogate of Section~\ref{sec:surrogate} also controls since
$\E(\bar\bq^\top\bM\bk')^2=\bar\bq^\top\bM\Sk'\bM\bar\bq\le\norm{\bar\bq}^2\tr(\bM\Sk'\bM)$.
We use uncentered moments throughout.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
$\Jal$ is the systematic query--key correlation left in the discarded
subspace: if keys and queries are consistently aligned along a direction,
throwing that direction away biases every score. $\Jfl$ is residual query
energy times residual key energy, coupled through the shared discarded
directions; it is the error one would see even with no systematic alignment.

\subsection{Prior methods as degenerate cases}
\label{sec:degenerate}

\begin{proposition}[What prior bases minimize]
\label{prop:degenerate}
Over rank-$r$ projectors $\bP$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item[(a)] Key-PCA (Loki, SALS, Palu, SVDq) minimizes $\tr(\bM\Sk)$, which
  equals $\Jfl$ when $\Sq=\bI$; it ignores $\Jal$ entirely. Equivalently it
  minimizes $J$ under the two false assumptions $\bC=\mathbf{0}$ and
  isotropic queries.
  \item[(b)] SWAN minimizes $\tr(\bM\Sq)+\tr(\bM\Sk)$, an additive surrogate
  of the multiplicative $\Jfl$; it also ignores $\Jal$.
  \item[(c)] \method{} minimizes $\fro{\bM\bC}^2$, a surrogate that upper
  bounds $\Jal$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:bound}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) With $\Sq=\bI$, $\tr(\bM\bI\bM\Sk)=\tr(\bM^2\Sk)=\tr(\bM\Sk)$ because
$\bM^2=\bM$. Loki's basis is the top-$r$ eigenbasis of $\Sk$, which is the
minimizer of $\tr(\bM\Sk)$ by the Ky~Fan inequality. (b) and (c) are the
stated objectives of the respective methods; see Section~\ref{sec:surrogate}
for (c).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Product versus sum]
\label{prop:prodsum}
Suppose $\Sq$ and $\Sk$ commute, with common eigenbasis and eigenvalues
$\{a_i\}$, $\{b_i\}$. Among projectors diagonal in that basis that discard a
coordinate set $D$ with $|D|=d-r$,
\begin{equation}
  \Jfl=\tr(\bM\Sq\bM\Sk)=\sum_{i\in D}a_ib_i .
\end{equation}
The exact minimizer discards the coordinates with the smallest
\emph{products} $a_ib_i$; SWAN discards the smallest \emph{sums} $a_i+b_i$;
key-PCA discards the smallest $b_i$. These disagree exactly when the spectra
are imbalanced: a direction with large query energy but negligible key energy
is kept by SWAN and correctly discarded by the product rule.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the common eigenbasis $\bM=\diag(m_i)$ with $m_i\in\{0,1\}$,
$\Sq=\diag(a_i)$, $\Sk=\diag(b_i)$, so
$\tr(\bM\Sq\bM\Sk)=\sum_i m_i^2a_ib_i=\sum_{i\in D}a_ib_i$.
\end{proof}
The restriction to axis-aligned projectors is for illustration; the
unconstrained minimizer of $\Jfl$ need not be axis-aligned.

\subsection{The \method{} basis and its optimality}
\label{sec:surrogate}

\begin{theorem}[Eckart--Young for the alignment surrogate]
\label{thm:ey}
Over rank-$r$ orthonormal $\bU$, the surrogate
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:surrogate}
  \hat J(\bU)=\fro{\bM\bC}^2=\fro{(\bI-\bU\bU^\top)\bC}^2
\end{equation}
is minimized by $\bU^\star=$ the top-$r$ left singular vectors of $\bC$,
equivalently the top-$r$ eigenvectors of $\bC\bC^\top$. The minimum value is
$\sum_{i>r}\sigma_i(\bC)^2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\fro{\bM\bC}^2=\tr(\bC^\top\bM^2\bC)=\tr(\bC^\top\bC)-\tr(\bC^\top\bP\bC)
=\fro{\bC}^2-\tr(\bU^\top\bC\bC^\top\bU)$.
Minimizing $\hat J$ is therefore maximizing $\tr(\bU^\top\bC\bC^\top\bU)$
over orthonormal $\bU$. By the Ky~Fan maximum principle this is at most the
sum of the $r$ largest eigenvalues of $\bC\bC^\top$, attained when the
columns of $\bU$ span the corresponding eigenspace. The eigenvectors of
$\bC\bC^\top$ are the left singular vectors of $\bC$ and its eigenvalues are
$\sigma_i(\bC)^2$, which gives the minimum value.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The surrogate controls both alignment terms]
\label{prop:bound}
For any projector $\bM$ of rank $d-r$,
\begin{equation}
  |\tr(\bM\bC\bM\bC)|\le\fro{\bM\bC}^2,\qquad
  (\tr\bM\bC)^2\le(d-r)\fro{\bM\bC}^2,
\end{equation}
hence $\Jal\le(d-r+1)\fro{\bM\bC}^2$, and $\bU^\star$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:ey}
minimizes this bound exactly.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First inequality: $\tr(\bM\bC\bM\bC)=\tr\big((\bM\bC)(\bM\bC)\big)
=\ip{(\bM\bC)^\top}{\bM\bC}_F\le\fro{(\bM\bC)^\top}\fro{\bM\bC}=\fro{\bM\bC}^2$
by the trace Cauchy--Schwarz inequality.
Second: $\tr(\bM\bC)=\tr(\bM\cdot\bM\bC)=\ip{\bM}{\bM\bC}_F$, and
$\fro{\bM}^2=\tr\bM=d-r$, so $(\tr\bM\bC)^2\le\fro{\bM}^2\fro{\bM\bC}^2=(d-r)\fro{\bM\bC}^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact optimality under isotropic fluctuations]
\label{prop:iso}
If $\Sq=\sigma_q^2\bI$ and $\Sk=\sigma_k^2\bI$ then
$\Jfl=\sigma_q^2\sigma_k^2(d-r)$ is constant in $\bU$, so minimizing $J$ is
minimizing $\Jal$ alone. If in addition $\bC$ is symmetric positive
semidefinite, the top-$r$ eigenbasis of $\bC$ minimizes $J$ exactly.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\tr(\bM\sigma_q^2\bI\bM\sigma_k^2\bI)=\sigma_q^2\sigma_k^2\tr(\bM^2)=\sigma_q^2\sigma_k^2(d-r)$.
If $\bC=\bC^\top\succeq0$ with eigenvalues $c_1\ge\dots\ge c_d\ge0$, let
$\mu_1\ge\dots\ge\mu_{d-r}$ be the eigenvalues of the compression $\bM\bC\bM$
restricted to $\mathrm{range}(\bM)$. Then $\tr(\bM\bC)=\sum_i\mu_i$ and, using
$\bM^2=\bM$ and cyclicity, $\tr(\bM\bC\bM\bC)=\tr\big((\bM\bC\bM)^2\big)=\sum_i\mu_i^2$.
By Cauchy interlacing, $\mu_i\ge c_{r+i}$ for every $i$, so both sums are at
least their values at the axis-aligned choice that discards the $d-r$
smallest eigen-directions, and that choice attains them. Hence the top-$r$
eigenbasis of $\bC$ minimizes $\Jal$, and since $\bC$ is symmetric PSD its
left singular vectors are its eigenvectors, so this is the \method{} basis.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Regime prediction]
\label{cor:regime}
When $\fro{\bC}^2$ is large relative to $\tr(\Sq)\tr(\Sk)/d$ (large $\rho_h$),
$\Jal$ dominates $J$ and the \method{} basis is near-optimal. When $\rho_h$ is
small, $\Jfl$ dominates and the optimal basis moves toward
coupled-auto-covariance structure. The advantage of \method{} over key-PCA is
therefore predicted to increase with $\rho_h$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[Loki is the self-collapse of \method{}]
\label{lem:collapse}
If queries are replaced by keys in the calibration statistic, i.e.\
$\bC\leftarrow\E[\bk\bk^\top]=\Sk$, then the \method{} basis equals the key-PCA
basis.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\Sk$ is symmetric PSD, so its left singular vectors are its eigenvectors
ordered by eigenvalue, which is the PCA basis.
\end{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} is a useful unit test: pointing the calibration code
at key tensors on both sides must reproduce Loki's components bit-for-bit.

\subsection{Grouped-query attention}
\label{sec:gqa}

Under GQA, one key head serves $G$ query heads, so there are $G$
cross-covariances $\bC_g=\E[\bk\,\bq^{(g)\top}]$ per KV head. Loki stores one
basis per KV head. A fair comparison must do the same.

\begin{theorem}[GQA pooling is exactly surrogate-optimal]
\label{thm:gqa}
Define the group-summed surrogate $\hat J_{\mathrm{grp}}(\bU)=\sum_{g=1}^G\fro{\bM\bC_g}^2$.
It is minimized by the top-$r$ eigenvectors of
$\mathbf{A}=\sum_{g=1}^G\bC_g\bC_g^\top$, equivalently the top-$r$ left
singular vectors of the horizontally stacked matrix
$[\bC_1\,|\,\bC_2\,|\cdots|\,\bC_G]\in\R^{d\times Gd}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\sum_g\fro{\bM\bC_g}^2=\fro{\bM[\bC_1|\cdots|\bC_G]}^2$ since the Frobenius
norm of a block-row matrix is the sum over blocks. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:ey} to the stacked matrix; its left singular vectors are the
eigenvectors of $[\bC_1|\cdots|\bC_G][\bC_1|\cdots|\bC_G]^\top=\sum_g\bC_g\bC_g^\top$.
\end{proof}

This promotes GQA pooling from a heuristic to the exact solution of the
group-summed problem, and it gives \method{} the same basis count and storage
as Loki. It also contrasts with SALS's joint-head lemma, which argues only
that stacking key auto-covariances across heads captures at least as much key
energy; Theorem~\ref{thm:gqa} is a statement about score error and respects
the GQA score structure (each $\bq^{(g)}$ really does score against the same
$\bk$).

\begin{remark}[Per-query-head upper bound]
Fitting a separate basis $\bU_g$ from each $\bC_g$ minimizes each
$\fro{\bM_g\bC_g}^2$ individually and therefore lower-bounds
$\hat J_{\mathrm{grp}}$; but it requires $G$ latent copies of every key. We
report it as an oracle to show how much the shared constraint costs
(Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Pre-RoPE closed form and the oracle basis}
\label{sec:oracle}

Before RoPE, $\bq^{(g)}=\bW_q^{(g)}\bx$ and $\bk=\bW_k\bx$ are linear in the
same hidden state $\bx\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}$ with second moment
$\Sx=\E[\bx\bx^\top]$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:closedform}
  \begin{aligned}
  \bC_g&=\bW_k\Sx\bW_q^{(g)\top},&
  \Sq^{(g)}&=\bW_q^{(g)}\Sx\bW_q^{(g)\top},\\
  \Sk&=\bW_k\Sx\bW_k^\top,&
  \mathbf{A}&=\bW_k\Sx\Big(\sum_g\bW_q^{(g)\top}\bW_q^{(g)}\Big)\Sx\bW_k^\top .
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Every term of \eqref{eq:wick} is therefore a known function of the weights
and a single $d_{\mathrm{model}}\times d_{\mathrm{model}}$ calibration
statistic. This has two uses.

\paragraph{Oracle basis.}
The full group-summed objective
$J_{\mathrm{grp}}(\bU)=\sum_g\big[(\tr\bM\bC_g)^2+\tr(\bM\bC_g\bM\bC_g)+\tr(\bM\Sq^{(g)}\bM\Sk)\big]$
is a smooth function on the Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}(r,d)$. At $d=128$ it
can be minimized offline per KV head by Riemannian gradient descent
(Appendix~\ref{app:oracle}) in seconds. We call the result the
\emph{$J$-opt oracle}. It is deployable (it is just another fixed basis), and
it lets us report what fraction of the Loki$\to$oracle gap each closed-form
basis closes. If \method{} closes most of it, the closed form is the right
headline; if not, the oracle itself is the method.

\paragraph{Sanity check of the surrogate.}
Because \eqref{eq:closedform} needs only $\Sx$, we can evaluate $J$ for any
candidate basis and check that the Gaussian-surrogate ranking of bases agrees
with the empirical score MSE $\frac1N\sum_n(\bq_n^\top\bM\bk_n)^2$ and with
downstream recall. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports this agreement.

\section{\method{}: Algorithm and Cost}
\label{sec:method}

\method{} has the same two phases as Loki. Only the statistic diagonalized in
the offline phase changes.

\subsection{Offline calibration}

For each layer and each KV head we run the model on a calibration corpus of
$N$ tokens (Section~\ref{sec:setup_exp}) and accumulate, for each query head
$g$ in the group, $\bC_g=\frac1N\bK^\top\bQ^{(g)}$. We form
$\mathbf{A}=\sum_g\bC_g\bC_g^\top$, take its eigendecomposition
$\mathbf{A}=\bU_{\mathrm{full}}\bm{\Lambda}\bU_{\mathrm{full}}^\top$ with
eigenvalues in descending order, and store the complete orthogonal matrix
$\bU_{\mathrm{full}}\in\R^{d\times d}$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:offline}). Storing
all $d$ columns, as Loki does, keeps the online rotation exact
(Lemma~\ref{lem:exact}): the first $r$ columns give the latent scores and the
full rotation gives exact scores on the selected tokens.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\method{} offline calibration (one layer, one KV head)}
\label{alg:offline}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require calibration states $\bK\in\R^{N\times d}$, $\bQ^{(1..G)}\in\R^{N\times d}$ taken at the point where they enter the dot product
\State $\mathbf{A}\gets\mathbf{0}_{d\times d}$
\For{$g=1,\dots,G$}
  \State $\bC_g\gets\frac1N\bK^\top\bQ^{(g)}$ \Comment{cross-covariance, $d\times d$}
  \State $\mathbf{A}\gets\mathbf{A}+\bC_g\bC_g^\top$
\EndFor
\State $(\bm{\Lambda},\bU_{\mathrm{full}})\gets\mathrm{eigh}(\mathbf{A})$, columns sorted by descending eigenvalue
\State \textbf{assert} $\bU_{\mathrm{full}}^\top\bU_{\mathrm{full}}=\bI$ (orthonormality)
\State \textbf{emit} $\bU_{\mathrm{full}}$ and metadata \{rotary type, calibration set, $G$\}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\method{} online step (one query head; identical to Loki's)}
\label{alg:online}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require basis $\bU_{\mathrm{full}}$ of the head's KV group, scoring rank $r$, budget $k$
\Require rotated key cache $\hat\bK=\bK\bU_{\mathrm{full}}\in\R^{S\times d}$ (maintained incrementally), values $\bV$
\State $\hat\bq\gets\bU_{\mathrm{full}}^\top\bq$ \Comment{$O(d^2)$}
\State $\tilde{\mathbf{s}}\gets\hat\bK_{:,1:r}\,\hat\bq_{1:r}$ \Comment{latent scores, $O(Sr)$}
\State $\mathcal{T}\gets\mathrm{topk}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}},k)$
\State $\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{T}}\gets\hat\bK_{\mathcal{T},:}\,\hat\bq/\sqrt d$ \Comment{exact scores, $O(kd)$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:exact}}
\State $\mathbf{y}\gets\mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{T}})\,\bV_{\mathcal{T},:}$ \Comment{$O(kd)$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Online selection}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:online} is Loki's algorithm verbatim. Each new key is
rotated once when written to the cache; each query is rotated once per step.
Latent scores use the first $r$ rotated coordinates of every cached key;
exact scores on the top-$k$ use all $d$ rotated coordinates. Because
$\bU_{\mathrm{full}}$ is orthogonal, the exact scores equal $\bq^\top\bk_j$
and no reconstruction is needed. Values are untouched.

\subsection{Cost analysis}
\label{sec:cost}

Let $S$ be the cache length, $d$ the head dimension, $r$ the scoring rank and
$k$ the number of selected tokens, and count multiply--adds and bytes moved
per query head per decode step (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). Full attention reads
$2Sd$ elements and does $2Sd$ multiply--adds. Loki and \method{} read
$Sr+2kd$ elements and do $Sr+2kd$ multiply--adds plus $2d^2$ for the two
rotations, which is independent of $S$. With Loki's standard settings
$r/d=1/4$ and $k/S=1/8$ the data-movement ratio is
$(r/d+2k/S)/2=(0.25+0.25)/2=1/4$.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Per-head, per-step cost. \method{} and Loki are identical by construction.}
\label{tab:cost}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
 & Elements read & Multiply--adds & Extra state \\
\midrule
Full attention & $2Sd$ & $2Sd$ & -- \\
Loki           & $Sr+2kd$ & $Sr+2kd+2d^2$ & $\bU_{\mathrm{full}}$ ($d^2$ per KV head) \\

Because the two methods share layouts and kernels, we do not claim any
throughput advantage over Loki, and we reuse Loki's Triton kernels unchanged.
The systems claim of this paper is that the selection family delivers real
decode speedup at long context (already established by Loki); the accuracy
claim is that \method{} achieves better recall and perplexity at the same
compression ratio. We keep these two claims separate.

\subsection{Handling rotary embeddings}
\label{sec:rope}

RoPE is a position-dependent rotation applied to $\bq$ and $\bk$ before the
dot product. Two design points follow.

\paragraph{Fit/inference alignment is mandatory.}
If the basis is fitted on pre-RoPE states, it must be applied to pre-RoPE
states; if fitted post-RoPE, applied post-RoPE. A mismatch destroys recall
(Section~\ref{sec:results}; we observed near-random recall in an early run
with a pre-RoPE basis applied to post-RoPE keys). Algorithm~\ref{alg:offline}
records the rotary type in the emitted metadata and the loader refuses a
mismatch.

\paragraph{Post-RoPE is the default for selection.}
Post-RoPE $\bC$ averages the rotation over positions in the calibration set.
Its top singular directions are therefore the directions along which
queries and keys stay aligned \emph{after} RoPE, which is what the deployed
dot product sees. The price is a higher effective rank, as Loki also found.
The pre-RoPE alternative, used by SALS and ShadowKV for storage, keeps rank
low but requires applying RoPE after selection and raises the question of
whether pre-RoPE subspaces transfer across sequences at long context. We
isolate the two factors with the $2\times2$ experiment
$\{\text{pre},\text{post}\}\times\{\Sk,\bC\}$ in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{What \method{} is not}
\label{sec:notcompress}

The \method{} basis is a \emph{selection} basis, not a \emph{storage} basis.
Storing only the rank-$r$ latents $\hat\bK_{:,1:r}$ and using the latent
scores as final attention logits fails badly: since
$\bq^\top\bP\bk=(\bU^\top\bq)^\top(\bU^\top\bk)$, this is exactly the
selection score used as the final logit, and $\bU$ was chosen to capture
alignment directions, not key energy, so peak logits shrink and the softmax
flattens. Softmax is sensitive to score \emph{magnitudes}, not just their
order. If memory compression is wanted, the correct design is two bases:
store in a key-energy basis (Loki/SALS) and select with \method{}; we leave
this to future work.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Surrogate assumptions.} The decomposition of
Theorem~\ref{thm:wick} closes the fourth moment with a Gaussian assumption
and targets the pre-softmax score. We validate both bridges empirically
(surrogate ranking agrees with empirical score MSE; score MSE tracks recall,
relL2 and perplexity), but neither is a theorem about the end task.

\paragraph{Selection, not compression.} \method{} keeps the full KV cache.
Section~\ref{sec:notcompress} explains why the selection basis cannot double
as a storage basis; a two-basis design that stores in a key-energy basis and
selects with \method{} is the natural extension.

\paragraph{Long-context transfer.} Post-RoPE bases average over calibration
positions. Whether they, or pre-RoPE bases, transfer to sequences much longer
than the calibration length is an open question raised by ShadowKV; our
$2\times2$ experiment is at \TODO{length}.

\paragraph{Dynamic bases.} $\bC$ is a corpus-level statistic. A per-sequence
or per-prefix $\bC$ would adapt to the prompt at the cost of an online SVD.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The basis that sparse attention uses to shrink queries and keys should be
chosen for the scores it preserves, not for the keys it reconstructs. We
made this precise by decomposing the expected squared score error into an
alignment term and a fluctuation term, showed that key-PCA minimizes a
degenerate case of the latter and ignores the former, and proposed
\method{}, whose basis is the top left singular subspace of the query--key
cross-covariance and is surrogate-optimal for the alignment term, with an
exactly optimal GQA-pooled form. Changing this one offline statistic, and
nothing else, gives \TODO{summary of gains} at identical runtime cost.

\section{Additional Derivations}
\label{app:derivations}

\subsection{Relation between score error and top-$k$ membership}
\label{app:margin}
Let $s_j$ be true scores, $\tilde s_j=s_j-\epsilon_j$ approximate scores with
$\epsilon_j=\bq^\top\bM\bk_j$, and $\tau$ the $k$-th largest true score. A
token $j$ with $s_j\ge\tau$ is dropped only if some token $i$ with $s_i<\tau$
satisfies $\tilde s_i>\tilde s_j$, which requires
$\epsilon_j-\epsilon_i>s_j-s_i\ge s_j-\tau+\tau-s_i$. Hence a wrong swap
between $j$ and $i$ needs $|\epsilon_j|+|\epsilon_i|>$ their combined margin
to $\tau$. By Markov's inequality on $\epsilon_j^2$, the probability that
token $j$'s error exceeds a margin $m_j$ is at most $J/m_j^2$ under the
calibration distribution, which is how $J$ enters ranking error.

\subsection{Frobenius surrogate and the alignment terms: tightness}
Equality in Proposition~\ref{prop:bound}'s first inequality holds when
$\bM\bC$ is symmetric; in the second when $\bM\bC\propto\bM$ on
$\mathrm{range}(\bM)$. Both hold for symmetric PSD $\bC$ in its eigenbasis,
which is why Proposition~\ref{prop:iso} is exact there.

\subsection{Whitened variant}
\label{app:whiten}
The exact Eckart--Young statement for the score $\bq^\top\bk$ under general
$\Sq,\Sk$ is obtained by whitening: with
$\tilde\bC=\Sk^{-1/2}\bC\Sq^{-1/2}$, the best rank-$r$ approximation of
$\tilde\bC$ minimizes $\E[(\bq^\top\bk-\tilde s)^2]$ over \emph{all}
bilinear rank-$r$ forms, not only shared orthogonal projectors. The resulting
key- and query-side maps differ, which brings back the asymmetric
full-rank problem of Lemma~\ref{lem:exact} unless the maps are re-orthonormalized.

\section{Oracle Optimization on the Grassmannian}
\label{app:oracle}
We minimize $J_{\mathrm{grp}}(\bU)$ over $\bU^\top\bU=\bI_r$ using the
pre-RoPE closed forms of \eqref{eq:closedform}. Gradient with respect to $\bM$
(treating $\bM$ as a free symmetric matrix, then projecting):
\begin{align}
\nabla_{\bM}(\tr\bM\bC)^2 &= 2(\tr\bM\bC)\,\bC^\top,\\
\nabla_{\bM}\tr(\bM\bC\bM\bC) &= \bC\bM\bC+\bC^\top\bM\bC^\top,\\
\nabla_{\bM}\tr(\bM\Sq\bM\Sk) &= \Sq\bM\Sk+\Sk\bM\Sq .
\end{align}
Since $\bM=\bI-\bU\bU^\top$, $\nabla_{\bU}J=-2\,\mathrm{sym}(\nabla_{\bM}J)\,\bU$.
